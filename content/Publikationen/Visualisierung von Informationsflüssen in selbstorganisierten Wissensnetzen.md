## Abstract (English)

This presentation examines learning processes in self-organizing knowledge networks (SOKNs) such as weblogs and wikis, contrasting them with traditional hierarchical learning environments and early e-learning approaches. While classical education relies on a clear teacher–learner distinction, fixed curricula, and intentional knowledge transfer, SOKNs are characterized by fluid roles, emergent collaboration, and learning as a network effect — a phenomenon the authors term "ambididactics." Drawing on examples from Wikipedia and the blogosphere, the presentation analyzes how social conventions crystallize into technological infrastructure (e.g., the blogroll), how distinct roles emerge (hubs, trendsetters, compressors, gurus), and how different "cultures of communication" — reaffirmative vs. innovative — exhibit fundamentally different network dynamics. The authors argue that visualizing information flows and network structures from a bird's-eye perspective can provide both scientific insights into the dynamics of these networks and practical tools that help individual actors navigate and participate more effectively in self-organized knowledge environments.

## Abstract (Deutsch)

Dieser Vortrag untersucht Lernprozesse in selbstorganisierten Wissensnetzen (SOKNs) wie Weblogs und Wikis und stellt sie traditionellen hierarchischen Lernumgebungen sowie frühen E-Learning-Ansätzen gegenüber. Während klassische Bildung auf einer klaren Lehrer-Lerner-Unterscheidung, fixen Curricula und intentionalem Wissenstransfer beruht, zeichnen sich SOKNs durch fließende Rollen, emergente Kollaboration und Lernen als Netzwerkeffekt aus — ein Phänomen, das die Autoren als "Ambididaktik" bezeichnen. Anhand von Beispielen aus Wikipedia und der Blogosphäre analysiert der Vortrag, wie soziale Konventionen zu technologischen Infrastrukturen kristallisieren (z. B. die Blogroll), wie sich unterschiedliche Rollen herausbilden (Hubs, Trendsetter, Kompressoren, Gurus) und wie verschiedene "Kommunikationskulturen" — reaffirmativ vs. innovativ — grundlegend unterschiedliche Netzwerkdynamiken aufweisen. Die Autoren argumentieren, dass die Visualisierung von Informationsflüssen und Netzwerkstrukturen aus der Vogelperspektive sowohl wissenschaftliche Einblicke in die Dynamik dieser Netzwerke ermöglicht als auch praktische Werkzeuge bereitstellt, die einzelnen Akteuren helfen, in selbstorganisierten Wissensumgebungen zu navigieren und effektiver zu partizipieren.

- Let's have a look at some examples in order to better appreciate the differences between hierarchical learning and SOKNs:

  

25.

  

- Wikipedia: Wikipedia shows that sharing knowledge in a self-organized fashion works very well in practice. Everyone can participate in the production of knowlegde - and there's a system of collaborative, decentralized [collective] quality assurance in place.

  

26.

  

- Recent statistical analyses show that there are 4 different groups of users:

  

1. Consumers

2. Occasional editors: Small corrections

3. Specialist authors/experts: Create new articles, only active on a limited number of articles (1-7) - their field of expertise.

4. Quality assurance "worker bees" (~ 1000). Apply corrections and make articles coherent. They assure that content is fact-checked at least on a superficial level (references), articles conform to Wikipedia style and layout, are correctly associated with categories &c.

  

27.

  

- [? really?] There's some rivalry between experts and the quality assurance people.

- The latter are generally more visible, because they're permanently on the case.

- Expert authors often have short bursts of activity - they work on (some) articles in their fields of expertise; aren't represented (or, I'd guess, even interested) in Wikipedia committees.

  

28.

  

- Nevertheless, the system works surprisingly well. In most cases articles converge to a relatively stable state.

- When it comes to controversial topics, however, it's very difficult to arrive at a universally acceptable version; from the viewpoint of classical education that's [potentially] problematic because of the lack of clear-cut, definite explanations, definitions and descriptions ("edit wars").

- For ample demonstration, see the discussion relating to articles like "George Bush".

  

29.

  

- But actually that's not a "problem" at all.

- It just shows that learning isn't always just the reception of commonly accepted content, but also comprises the perception of discursive/conversational dynamics.

- The learner perceives that there's not always a universally accepted interpretation of reality and that shared knowledge undergoes a constant process of change through discourse.

- Importantly, it becomes readily apparent that the production of knowledge is a shared enterprise ready to be taken part in (as soon as one's position is established).

  

- Polarized [emotionally charged] opinions are usually more instructional [chl: don't know - certainly they're usually more interesting] than a mere assembly of facts presented in a stoic fashion.

- Even a level-headed religion as buddhism values disputation in the educational process of monks.

- A number of individual musicians [soloists] is dedicated to a theme and deals with it collectively.

- Looking closer, all parties involved collaboratively create a collective learning environment.

- This results in a win-win situation, in which everyone involved profits more than if he/she'd act isolated from the others.

Ambididactics - Learning as network effect

  

- This state of affairs seems to be best summarized by the term "ambididactics".

- No learning environment planned by a teacher of didact, no autodidact isolation [?], but rather an emergent system, in which there's no functional distinction between teachers and students.

- Learning happens as network effect, even though it wasn't planned and intended explicitly.

in effect, it's a hybrid form.

35.

  

An Example

  

- A so-called "blogroll" shows, what other weblogs an author considers as interesting.

- Somebody introduced that convention. Now, some years later, just about every weblog software comes with a feature to easily build such blogrolls, and most authors make use of it.

- We see the metamorphosis from social convention to technological feature - which in turn increased the probability that authors maintain blogrolls dramatically.

- (We might call that ”institutionalization of conventions through technology”. This form of institutionalization supports sustainable development [?].)

  

37.

  

- In that fashion, new social structures and forms of learning are created. A structural difference is especially apparent in the case of weblogs:

- They form a loose network rather than a delimitable community.

- What's the difference between "communities" and networks?

  

  

39.

  

- Communities define themselves through borders [? Grenzen], not only in the case of electronic media.

- Either you're a member or not.

- Communities also tend to represent roles and their functions explicitly. This makes navigating social space easier and potentially more efficient.

  

40.

  

- "Weblogs" as a whole are no "community" in the traditional sense.

- Members don't know each other (at least that's not a precondition).

- Very open, no impermeable borders.

- No "membership", no [central] organization.

- Based on conventions much more than on dedicated infrastructure.

  

48.

  

- SOKNs are rather loose networks.

- The lack of "borders" makes it hard to clearly distinguish regions.

- Connections between actors are much more dynamic than in communities.

- Fields of interest change and thereby relations and linkage structures between actors.

- Forms of temporary collaboration; when fields of interest change or restructure those collaborations fade.

- Actors don't have an overview of the whole system (of course), just of a limited neighbourhood (temporary cluster of like-minded people).

- Through individuals' behaviour (which is driven by individuals' personal goals) and the fact that many actors are connected to each other network effects occur which individuals aren't conscious of and rarely perceive.

- Only by leaving the perspective of the individual behind and gaining a bird's eye view it becomes visible how the network works.

65ff.

  

Distinct cultures of communication define themselves through distinct systemic dynamics.

  

- Re-affirmative [self-reinforcing?] vs. innovative culture of communication

  

RCC:

  

- few large/important nodes, lots of small ones, few medium-sized

- the large remain large, the small small

- total number remains more or less constant

  

ICC:

  

- sizes are evenly distributed

- importance of nodes varies over time

- total number varies much more

  

RCC:

  

- cluster formation remains constant

  

ICC:

  

- cluster formation is much more dynamic

- new clusters emerge (when new topics come up, new communities of interest are created on-the-fly)

- clusters vanish (when interest in a topic decreases)
