## Content

### A Game Based on Rating

To give the better impression of how the cooperation in rather complex systems may be designed, following *game* was invented. The term *game* is used because of the game-like rules. Rules can be seen as automatic relations between the amount of rating units someone has collected and his/her role in the system. Please note that it is not a game in the sense of the game-theory, but in the sense of party-games. Nevertheless it alludes to real social settings and mechanisms.

The game introduced here is only one of thousands possible games that may be designed for communities and its main objective is to discuss the impact of different rules on the development of communities. To give a better understanding of the game I embedded it into a real life scenario:

Imagine a trans-disciplinary context of research, like for example "Artificial Intelligence"(AI) or "Human-Computer-Interaction"(HCI). In such contexts of knowledge production there is a rather rapid change of themes and opinion leaders. The role structure of a messaging-system should mirror that by allowing maximum flexibility in opening new discussions and in supporting a permanent flow of the members' degree of activity between being active and organizing and being a rather passive consumer.

In other words: Roles and social funtions of a person may vary a lot. There are periods of high activity, when someone is eager to cooperate intensively and there are periods of lesser activity when someone prefers to lean back and to retire. The assignment of real-life-roles is therefore more or less soft and fluent.

The idea behind the following game is that teamwork depends very much on both a clear definition of roles as on the members' flexibility to change roles and to perform other functions. Hierarchy and the will to remain "on top" mostly interfere with common goals or impede in defining such goals. Therefore a game with dynamic hierarchies is introduced. A number of rules is avoiding that a person remains on top and is therefore getting exhausted and hindering others with fresh forces. It may be assumed that people will accept rotation as something very natural and positive for everybody.

Players may hold three different roles shown in the next figure.

![[ratinggame1.gif]]

Newbies are newcomers that become normal fellows after a certain time (depending on rules that will be defined later). Fellows and directing fellows match the two modes of activities described above. Members are to rotate between the roles of a normal fellow and a directing fellow. Figure 3 shall give an impression how newbies, fellows and directing fellows may be grouped into several discussions.

![[ratinggame2.gif]]

Normally newbies start in open discussions, that cope with very general and very new topics (like discussions E and F in Figure 3). Only newbies that have been personally invited by fellows or directing fellows may enter a closed discussion (like A, B or C in Figure 3). Closed discussions deal with more specific topics. Fellows and directing fellows may enter all closed discussions.

**The function of rating in the game-context**

In this context rating performs two functions:

1. interesting and outstanding contributions get marked (orientation function)
2. the role of people change according to the ammount of rating units they have collected by writing interesting and outstanding contributions. (role-dynamic function)

![[ratinggame3.gif]]

Figure 4 shows different forms of rating units to rate contributions. Everybody (also newbies) may rate with *flowers*. Special points named *fruits* are rating units reserved to directing fellows. *Fruits* express appreciation of a directing fellow and are a special honor to receive. E.g. directing fellows of the discussion A in Figure 3 may rate outstanding contributions with an acorn:

![[ratinggame4.gif]]

The different kinds of fruits belong to directing fellows in different discussions. The directing fellows of discussion B may only rate with shells. Directing fellows of discussion A use acorns. Acorns, shells, like any *fruit*, can not only be given to contributions in discussion A but to any contribution in the whole system, as there might be interesting ideas also outside a discussion. When discovering an acorn outside of discussion A, one knows that there is a very interesting idea in respective to the topics of discussion A.

![[ratinggame5.gif]]

All ratings are collected in the contributions account. Figure 5 shows that there may be of course different persons rating the same contribution.

![[ratinggame6.gif]]

The sum of ratings that belong to all contributions of a certain person are collected in his or her personal account. The *contributions account* orients the reader about the quality of the content, the *personal account* is important for a person's status within the system.

**Rule 1:** One to three flowers can be given at once. Giving flowers doesn't affect the donators own account of flowers.

**Rule 2:** One to three fruits may be given by directing fellows. Giving fruits reduces the donators account. Fruits of all kind can be converted into each other. E.g. if a fellow from discussion A has obtained two shells, three acorns and one cactus his account of fruits is six. Therefore he is allowed to give six acorns after becoming a directing fellow (because of rule 5) in discussion A.

*How to become a fellow?*

**Rule 3:** A newbie becomes a fellow by getting any fruit from a directing fellow.

**Rule 4:** A newbie gets three flowers from at least two different persons.

*How to become a directing fellow?*

**Rule 5:** After obtaining three fruits of one kind a fellow becomes a directing fellow in the respective discussion. Example: After getting three acorns a fellow becomes directing fellow in discussion A (in figure 3). It is possible to be a directing fellow in different discussions.

**Rule 6:** After spending all his fruits he returns to be an ordinary fellow.

These six basic rules are very simple, but nevertheless raise a great variety of possible behaviours and tactics by the players that need further discussion and additional rules, which is provided in the next chapter.

### Tuning the System

*Getting and loosing access*

It`s quite easy to become a fellow: The rating mechanisms defined in rule 3 and 4 construct a kind of boundary that keeps unwanted poeple out. This coincides with what was stated above, that boundaries may raise the mutual benefit of members. There is a possibility for a "gang" of three people to by-pass rule 4 by giving themselves high ratings. This may be avoided by special rules but such "gangs" may also be regarded as inspiring and innovating. If their influence on the discussion is considered destructive, they may be thrown out by a special rule e.g.

**Rule 7a:** Two directing fellows are enough to throw a fellow out of the respective discussion. If there's only one directing fellow, that one has the right to ban fellows. Their fellowship is not influenced by being banned.

This is a rather authoritarian rule, that allows quick reaction against unwanted poeple. It could be changed into a more democratic rule, like

**Rule 7b:** The banning of people needs a voting procedure, where all members may take part. A simple majority is needed.

*Directing discussions*

It's quite easy to become a fellow but not that easy to become a directing fellow. As I have stated above there should be special rules avoiding that a directing fellow remains in that role for a life time. This may be acceptable in a traditional discipline but not in a context of very dynamic knowledge production. Rules already mentioned don't force directing fellows to spend their fruits. Therefore we have to define an additional one.

**Rule 8:** A directing fellow has to spend at least three fruits within one month. Recipients have to be ordinary fellows.

This rule has two implications. Firstly it forces directing fellows to spend their fruits and secondly it produces a new generation of directing fellows. Rule 8 is the key-rule that provides rotation as it is impossible to create a closed curcuit of fruits between directing fellows only.

One consequence of rule 8 might be that rotation takes only place between a limited number of persons (see figure 5).

![[ratinggame7.gif]]

Alberto is rating Lisa's contributions with his fruits untill she becomes a directing fellow. She is rewarding that by making him a directing fellow again. This kind of *rotation gang* may be avoided by additional rules but I don't think that it has to be counterproductive as it provides rotation anyway. And it is visible to everybody. People may complain about *rotation gangs*, or they may insist on a change of the rules or they simply leave the discussion. One effective measure against small rotation gangs could be an addition to rule 5:

**Rule 5.1:** To become an directing fellow you need at least three fruits of one kind from at least two different directing fellows.

To by-pass this rule you need even more cooperation and that may be a wanted side-effect.

*How new discussions emerge and evolve*

New discussions may emerge out of two major reasons:

1. Fellows and directing fellows try to keep newbies out. Therefore they set up an additonal discussion, that may compete with the original one. But this needs an additional rule like

**Rule 10:** It needs at least three persons to found a new discussion. Everyone, including newbies, may do so.

2. Another reason could be that there is a real need for new discussions, as new topics have arisen.

New discussions don't have directing fellows. They are more or less anarchic and they have to pass a phasis of self-definition and self-construction until they become regular discussions. For example discussions D, E and F in figure 3. The change from the provisional to regular discussions has to be defined as rules.

**Rule 11:** After a turnover of at least 30 flowers within a month a discussion becomes regular. The two participants that hold the biggest amount of flowers become directing fellows.

There might be another rule influencing the amount of directing fellows. If there is a lot of traffic in a discussion a bigger amount of directing fellows may be needed:

**Rule 12:** If the amount of contributions within a month exceeds 200 an additional directing fellow will be nominated. It is the fellow with the biggest amount of flowers.

There are no restrictions for inventing new rules that change the system's behaviour. All participants may discuss intensively the impact of new rules. Procedures for the introduction of new rules may be introduced as well.
