## Abstract

This study examines the development process of Web4Groups from a sociological perspective, viewing technology and technical artifacts as social facts. It combines two traditions: social studies of technology and software development methodology (HCI, CMC).

In dieser Technikgenese-Studie wird die Entstehung einer neuen Software (Web4Groups) als neues "Stück Wirklichkeit" nachvollziehbar. Es wird gezeigt wie ein neues Medium, durch Kommunikation (Meetings, Mailinglisten) und temporäre Artefakte (Konzepte, Prototypen) entsteht.

### 1. Approach

This is – of course – only one out of many possible ways to examine the development process of Web4Groups. The way it was done here is strongly related to the role of this study within the project itself. This work is not only dedicated to the scientific-community but also to all members of the Web4Groups team. One of main objectives of this study is to improve co-operation within the team and to support Web4Groups development as a tool for group co-operation.

Facing these special requirements, we have tried to combine **two traditions** – two different ways of looking at technology.

The **first** derives from social studies of technology. We want to look at technology and technical artifacts as social facts. From this point of view technology and artifacts are more than their physical and material appearance: They are visible tracks of social interaction that underlay social processes of construction, like variation, negotiation, selection and affirmation. (see Rammert 1995). What makes this study different from classical "Technikgenese" is
1. that we look at these social processes from inside and not from outside.
2. that we look at them while they are going on and not afterwards and
3. that we try to influence the development of technology in an active way. Our aim is not to cover the whole field of telecommunication and try to make a "genesis of telematics" but to focus on the limited and very concrete Web4Groups-project, which is the target of our "sociological intervention".

Trying to follow such a new way of sociological work in the field of technology we could not neglect existing traditions of software development, as a **second** source of methodology. New fields of research like Human Computer Interaction (HCI) or Computer Mediated Communication (CMC) provide a lot of interesting approaches how to integrate the needs of individuals and groups in software development and how to manage it as a social process. However we missed a model of the development process that could be used as a tool to mediate conflicts or different interest in the process itself, somehow a tool for individuals for getting aware of the process and their own position within it.

The most adequate methodology has been developed by Christiane Floyd, who is looking at software development as *reality construction*. Each software-development project is opening a new area of reality, that is shaped by the participants of the development process. This new reality emerges out of a web of design-decisions. (Floyd 1992)

We want to look at Web4Groups as such a "new area of reality", that is produced by social interaction and that ends up in providing new artifacts but also new forms of interaction. The term that fits best to such a new "area of reality" seems to be the term "medium."

We define medium as a space of social interaction that is characterised
- by certain artifacts,
- by certain conventions for using these artifacts and
- by certain social settings, in which artifacts are modified or new ones are designed.

Each medium has a history and is evolving in a quasi-evolutionary cultural process that makes it different from its environment and that produces its own forms of internal communication as well as its own forms of perception.

The very crucial point of this understanding of medium is that it does not separate technology production from technology usage but instead views it as two instances of the same cultural process.

![[genesis_W4G1.gif]]
**Figure 1**

This Figure illustrates that Web4Groups as a medium consists of both, contexts of application as well as contexts of design. Both interfere with each other and coevolve in the long term. Contexts of application provide important inputs and experiences for contexts of design, which in turn develop new tools for practical usage.

It has been pointed out by different scholars (Jones 1997, Poster 1997, Latzer 1997), that new media should not be characterised by the "channel" or "highway"-metaphor suggesting that a medium's function only consists in linking a person A with a person B without affecting the content of what is communicated. Most scholars suggest to look at new media as a "space of communication" or the like. According to what we mentioned above, we want to see Web4Groups as a space of interactions that uses and produces artifacts which in turn shapes interactions. In other words artifacts seem to be the visible and more stable tracks within a network of communication and interaction and therefore we want to focus on them. In our model of the genesis of Web4Groups we roughly distinguish three kinds of artifacts:

![[genesis_W4G2.gif]]
**Figure 2: Different classes of artifacts**

*First* of all there are artifacts that were developed outside of the project but that perform important functions in setting-up and in running it. Such *ready-made artifacts*, had to be only adopted to special needs and circumstances of Web4Groups. Examples of such *ready-made artifact* are browsers and other software, telephones, books and so on.

*Second:* *Product artifacts*, i.e. results of the development process that will be presented to customers as well as investors. In Web4Groups this class of artifacts consists of several software-pieces like: The Web4Groups-server, the http-server, several pieces of software for advanced functionality (joint editing, voting, multi-language support, voice and fax-gateway, etc.) as well as user support like help and tutorial and finally one could also name here marketing strategies and consulting and training-concepts for organisations that want to use Web4Groups.

The *third* class of artifacts is the very focus of this study as it is the missing link between *ready-mades* and *products.* This artifacts can be regarded as important manifestations of design-communication. We suggest to call them therefore *artifacts of design*. The life-cycle of such artifacts is rather short as they only fulfil a function within the project, like visualising ideas, animating discussions, testing of contrary conceptions and so on. Examples in Web4Groups: the board-metaphor, scenarios, prototypes, reports and deliverables.

Design takes place in special defined situations. We call such defined situations, where design takes place *design-settings*. In Web4Groups design took place in settings like mailing lists, telephone conferences, meetings and so on.

*Contexts of application* will emerge when the software is released and when people start to use Web4Groups for their special purposes. In the current situation user-groups more or less simulated such contexts of application but concrete and realistic usage will certainly differ from that. However the current state fo the project doesn't allow to include contexts of application already in this study, but the concept is open enough to include them in future.

### 2. Method

It is important to mention that we accompanied the Web4Groups project throughout the last two years, since it started. The assessment-group at the Research Unit for Socio-Economics (Socoec) at the Austrian Academy of Science consisted of four persons that have been educated as social or cultural scientists, but the work in this project was much more then making studies like this one. We at Socoec were in charge of different activities like the co-ordination of user-groups, organisation of user-demonstrations, but we also were involved in user-interface design and some aspects of technical co-ordination. Finally we provided an expertise for the technical implementation of the voting tool. In a few words: our work has been manifold and exceeded ususal scientific work, but on the other hand this provided us with a lot of opportunities to talk to people and to collect experiences which an outsider could never gain. Certainly this deep involvement in the project itself finds expression in the way this study is done:

This study started with developing the model introduced above and with making some assumptions how design-settings and design-artifacts, ready-mades, and products look like from our point of view. This phase ended up in a draft version of this paper that was sent out to all team-members of Web4Groups. The paper included questions to be answered by the team-members in order to validate or to change our assumptions. It contained the following text:

*To all team-members of Web4Groups: ... Team members are invited to answer several questions as well as to modify descriptions according to their own point of view. Please regard the model introduced here more as a way to organise the discussion about project assessment and less as the true and only Web4Groups-story. See this model itself as a design-artifact that can be modified by you and that performs a certain function within the project: To assess internal and external communication and to provide a basis for better future co-operation. This model of the develoment process of Web4Groups is only valid to the extent that you confront it with your points of view and enrich it with your comments.*

In September 1997 at a management-meeting in Sophia-Antipolism that was attended by 13 team-members the most important topics could be discussed in a session of two hours. Passages from the discussion are added in this text to give a vivid expression of what people think and to what extent our assumptions are shared by others. Some parts of the study could be discussed in Sophia-Antipolis. We try to cover these parts with written answers to the draft version of the study (four persons answered) and by a lot of informal talks.

Conclusions were added in order to improve any kind of future co-operation.

### 3. Boundary-Conditions

Before we are going to describe artifacts and settings inside Web4Groups one has to look also at boundary-conditions in the environment, as they perform a certain influence. From our point of view such influence doesn't work in a deterministic manner, i.e. there is no automatic reaction to conditions from outside but the project has in most cases some options to react and tries to widen its own liberty of action in the long term. In the following we focus only on boundary-conditions that seemed to be most important.

#### EC, Competitiveness and User-Participation

It is obvious that not all projects are working under same conditions of competitiveness:

When a company like Microsoft is backing a software-project we can assume that the probability for success will be higher than in a relatively small EC-funded project like Web4Groups. It is trivial, but we have to state that power and money often dictate media development: In the field of operating-systems and word-processors on PCs Microsoft more or less dictates user-behaviour, as it holds the quasi-monopoly.

On the other hand, tools for the Internet especially web-based group-conferencing tools like Web4Groups are developed by a number of teams and individuals and there are a number of running systems. One can say the market for web-conferencing is highly-competitive and in such an environment user participation seems to be a conditio-sine-qua-non. The impact of user-participation has been realised by the EC's Telematic for Research Programme which forces all the projects to integrate users into system development and to set-up demonstration-phases for testing prototypes and products. In Web4Groups users were organised in a user-board to formulate user-needs and to influence management-decisions. One can say that user participation and the relatively high influence of the representatives of the user-groups in the management was influenced by the EC's policy and real market needs.

All participants of the discussion agreed that the EC's Telematic Application Programme especially influenced user participation:

> **Valentino Cavalli, project-manager:** *my opinion is not only based on Web4Groups but it seems to be a special requirements of telematic application programme projects. In telematic application programme there is a very strong requirement for a large user involvement. The special condition of the EC program affected very much this user orientation in Web4Groups. We have been free to choose four user groups instead of ten or two, but the special orientation towards verification and demonstration of the results and the involvement of user groups in all project phases - our choices have been strongly influenced by the conditions by the telematic application programme.*

> **Researcher:** *And did you think that it influenced the project in a positive way?*

> **Valentino Cavalli, project-manager:** *My opinion is: Even if this involvement had been very large in the project. I think it could have been much stronger than it was: One of the problems in the project was - to say it in a rude way - that we mostly showed bugs to the user and not results and how to use this results in real contexts. So I would say, the principle of user participation in Web4Groups is very good, but unfortunately the problem we had in respect to the technical issues - in my opinion - did not allow the users to have a real benefit from the project during the project life-time.*

> **Researcher:** *Do the user-groups have the same impression*

> **Alois Kempf, rep. of user-groups:** *I think this is to a large extent true, because when we came to the project we thought that after some month (when the gateway and the interface were finished) we could start with this tool and setting up services. - But I would like to mention the paradox situation which I see. I acknowledge that users had a loud voice at the begin of this project; and they got an echo for what they did, an echo on their formulation of user requirements. So many things have been changed due to user requirements and in this respect it was a very good telematic application programme project, because this was the stress of these projects in comparison to the framework three program. From this point of view there was a very large user involvement. - To come back to the paradox situation I see that the developers might rather go with high end users or with their colleagues in computer science. If one would have users only from computer science, the development team could keep to highest end of available technology. This however is not the reality for the groups, we would like to work with by using Web4Groups. Developers have to show that they are up-to-date with the new technology, on the other hand they have to serve low end users which we in the forest research community have throughout Europe. This is definitly a difficult situation: the developers have to present themselves as (technology) experts in an annual review but also to fulfil our requirements and expectations at the lower end of telematics technology.*

#### The Internet as a technological and cultural frame for Web4Groups

The Internet, or rather the WorldWideWeb had strong influence on the design of Web4Groups from our point of view. There were a number of restrictions to live with as well as a number of cultural and technical conventions to follow:

- Web4Groups should be accessible with any usual web-browser like Netscape Navigator, Internet Explorer, Mosaic and so on. Web4Groups should not be a browser independent client. It should be accessible like any other web-page. Therefore design was limited by the technical possibilities of browsers.
- Web4Groups had to follow graphical and other cultural conventions of the Web. HTML, the common markup language of the Web influences the way of possible interaction and interface design: e.g. Drag and Drop operations, which seem to be important for user-friendly design, are not supported by HTML.
- Other conventions of E-mail influenced Web4Groups: Terms like "subject line" or "body of the message" derive from E-mail as well as symbols like "flags" for unread messages

It turned out that a lot of team-members, especially developers, fear that Web4Groups is not keeping up with the actual technical developments on the Internet, especially as regards browser development:

> **Samba Diallo, developer:** *We do not keep up with browser development. Because as Jacob already said we don't use Java and we don't use Frames. There are a lot of things we could offer to users but we don't have it.*

> **Roland Alton-Scheidl, assessment-manager:** *I think the current situation is not very satisfying, on the one hand we use functions from browsers which are not available in all browsers, like cookies and Javascript, but we do not really benefit of what they could provide us as solutions.*

> **Jacob Palme, developer:** *The decision not to use frames and Javascript was based on how the world looked at that time - one and a half your ago. But today it looks very different. The decision not to use Frames and Java was made one and half years ago. The problem that you only look at how the world looks today and not how it will look like when the software is finished. (...) Java script and frames have been available in both, Netscape navigator and Internet Explorer for a very long time... and I think the mistake we made in the beginning was that we used scenario technique to describe the possible future of our project, but we did not user scenario technique to describe the future of the environment. If we had made such scenarios (to describe how the Internet would evolve during the forthcoming year) we would have understood that Java and Frame would become common tools which everyone had available.*

**Conclusion for future co-operation:** Rethink the use of Frames and Java for future releases of Web4Groups. Provide a scenario how Java could be used in Web4Groups in order to improve user-interaction. Provide general scenarios of the future technical and cultural development of the Internet especially of Internet browsers and WWW-services.

### 4. Ready-Made Artifacts

![[genesis_W4G3.gif]]
**Figure 3: Ready-made artifacts**

We want to call artifacts which are not produced by the design-process but which support it as prefabricated tools *ready-made artifacts*. All kind of hard- and software, used during the design-process and also telephones, office-commodities etc. belong to that kind of artifacts as well as immaterial artifacts like terms or theories obtained through books or via WWW. Like all other artifacts *ready-mades*' functions and meanings change depending on their context of use. Some artifacts were adopted to the specific needs of the projects: e.g.

- e-mail was not only used for long-distance communication but also for communication between two neighboured rooms that were only separated by a door.
- The WorldWideWeb was not only used for information-retrieval but also for making own documents accessible to all partners.
- Telephones was not only used for one-to-one communication but also for telephone-conferences.

One could say that *ready-mades* are incorporated bounary-conditions. They represent the state-of-the-art, skills and typical conventions that can be found in the environment of the project. But the usage of *ready-mades* is therefore not predefined. The team members can select from a bandwidth of usage and can invent their own ones. The way how *ready-mades* are used show if the design team is able to deal with boundary-conditions in a creative way. A creative usage of *ready-mades* widens the liberty of action.

The following provides a more detailed description of some important ready-mades and their usage in Web4Groups:

Internet-clients like Browsers, mailreaders, and ftp-software, fulfilled three important functions:
1st they served as a standard for own developments: Mailreaders and browsers themselves increasingly integrate groupware functionality and can therefore be seen as competitors.
2nd As a web-client Web4Groups needs a browser to be shown on the screen. Browser-functionality like file-upload and the limitations of HTML had strong influence in the design of Web4Groups. Browser-functionality changed a lot during the past two years and raised the need for design-decisions: How should the project react on new browser-features like "Frames" or "Java"?
3rd Finally Browsers, mailreaders and ftp-clients have been important means of project-communication. Most time E-mail, especially mailing lists formed the "backbone" of communication. Mailreaders and browsers strongly influenced the individual communication behaviour of some poeple. A lot of team-members learned the meaning of telematic by the project itself.

Also the KOM-Groupware-Server by DSV can be seen as ready-made. It was a more or less running piece of software before the project started and has played a central part in the whole Web4Groups architecture. The KOM-Server was an important starting point for system-design. But it turned out that it was not easy to merge the KOM-concept with the concept of Web4Groups

> **Jacob Palme, developer:** *I think that we were not successful in conveying the ideas of the KOM server to the other partners. And if we had been successful in this I think the KOM server would have been more useful. Many mistakes would have been avoided. I think it was a problem of us at DSV that we were not good enough at conveying to you our ideas.*

> **Samba Diallo, developer:** *We had no problems to understand the concept of KOM. The problem was that we had two different things. We had on the one side a system with a design that was implemented to a very large extent and on the other hand we had requirements. and some of those were conflicting (with was provided by the KOM-Server). We at Kapsch were a sort of interface between user requirements and KOM. What we did for the specification of the API was: Take the user requirements and check what is there and check what is in the KOM server.*

> **Researcher to Mr. Palme:** *What do you think was the main problem in convincing other project partners:*

> **Jacob Palme, developer:** *That the XVT-client didn't work. I think there are two things that we should have done: instead with the XVT-client we would have provided you with the Super-KOM system; it has an old fashioned interface but you would have understood the ideas of the KOM-System. The other thing is that we should have made some kind of tutorial where I went through the user interface and explain the basic ideas.*

> **Roland Alton-Scheidl, assessment-manager:** *I also tested SuperKOM and was astonished that it has such a neat telnet-interface.*

> **Researcher:** *Do you have now a full understanding of the KOM-System*

> **Samba Diallo, developer:** *Yes.*

**Conclusions for future co-operation:** Make the history of Web4Groups transparent to all new partners. Give all partners, especially DSV, the possibilities to present their influence in the development. Show which parts of Web4Groups derive from the old KOM-system. Demonstrate old KOM-Systems like SuperKOM. Show the differences between KOM and Web4Groups.

### 5. Product Artifacts

![[genesis_W4G4.gif]]
**Figure 4: Products**

From an internal point of view products establish external relations of design. Products are versions of software that are dedicated to the public, as well as reports and deliverables that inform the European Commission about the project's progress. Finally we may also subsume those artifacts that support the implementation of the software and cope with organisational and social aspects of software usage: help-system, tutorial, training-programmes and so on.

One can see products from two different perspectives:

From an **internal** perspective, products seem only to be a special instance of design-artifacts. Products are the result of design and it is a matter of discretion to publish it or not. Even after a product has been published it is never perfect. It is in the nature of iterative design that products are continuously refined. From that point of view products are nothing else than prototypes for the next generation of products.

Viewed from the **outside** products are some kind of interface between the medium and its environment. In most cases products are the only thing that can be judged from outside. The user is not interested in internal structures of communication, he wants a running product. The same is in many cases true for investors. The Commission seemed to be not interested in management-affairs as long as the results were satisfying. If a project doesn't succeed in publishing products within a certain period of time there is a growing risk to loose resources from outside.

By deciding that a product shall be released, external relations are intensified. Prototypes only underlay a controlled process of internal validation (as I am going to explain later) but the effects and the acceptance of products are less predictable. Products serve as a probe to explore reactions from outside and examine design assumptions and design-goals. Products can fail because of sometimes ridiculous reasons. Anyway, if the lessons from user- and market- reactions are learned one can change goals and methods of design.

Product artifacts in the Web4Groups project:

- Web4Groups-Server and http-server
- multi-language support
- joint editing
- voting (rating, annotation)
- e-mail-gateway
- replication system
- fax and voice -gateway
- help system
- tutorial
- consulting concepts
- marketing concepts
- marketing materials like folders and posters
- a book about the conference "Voting, Rating and Annotation"

Take a look at the Web4Groups pages at http://www.Web4Groups.at to get a more detailled information about the product-parts of Web4Groups.

> **Researcher:** *Do you think that the different products can be merged into one.*

> **Valentino Cavalli, project-manager:** *I want to add the we have to produce before the end of the project a project implementation plan. We have to identify project results and to say for each of them to what extent it can be provided to people outside, so what you raised is actually one of our commitments.*

### 6. Design-Artifacts

![[genesis_W4G5.gif]]
**Figure 5: Design-artifacts**

The outstanding importance of artifacts of design is based on their function for internal communication. Design-artifacts are the missing link between *ready-mades* and *products*. They help to visualise ideas and initiate discussions about the product-design. Whereas *ready-mades* and *products* limit and define the space of design, design artifacts explore and structure that space. *Ready-mades* and *products* depend very much on external factors, whereas *design artifacts* and *design settings* can be invented by the design-team itself and are therefore visible signs of autonomy. If one wants to improve design and to support internal communication, artifacts of design should be in the focus of reflection.

*Design artifacts* in the Web4Groups project:

#### Metaphors

Metaphors are a common knack of initiating discussions and visualising own thoughts. Let us have a closer look on how metaphors work: In Web4Groups the so called board-metaphor which was introduced by the assessment-manager has been an important input at the beginning of the design-process: He demonstrated his idea with a little blackboard made of slate. One could leave messages for others there and readers of that message could comment them. The meaning of board are very manifold but all of the different meanings seem to fit perfectly to a conferencing-tool. In the notion of notice-board it serves for public announcements. The term *bulletin-board* is already well known to on-line communities. Bulletin-boards are electronic means of message-exchange based on computers and telephone.

On the one hand the board metaphor was a means to visualise an object that can be used in a lot of different contexts. On the other side it opened a path for technical solutions. The board metaphor provided both: an imagination of future usage and an approach for its realisation - and that is a lot in the kick-off phase of a project. Whether one agreed with it or not, it was something one could talk about.

> **Samba Diallo, developer:** *I think it helped a lot in thinking and in reflecting on objects which have a lot of things in common, but I think we still have a lot of problems giving this board-concept to the users and to the interface.*

> **Roland Alton-Scheidl, assessment-manager:** *To say it correctly it was decided half a year ago not to use the board metaphor anymore in the interface. It was just a model - a design-artifact - as you would say - in the beginning for the set-up-phase to have a common understanding but it turned out e.g. that board cannot be translated to many languages correctly. .... You tried to translate it into Italian... but we didn't succeed in finding a graphical concept for it so we keep now to say this is a message, this is an activity this are users...this is fine as well. The board-metaphor helped us for a common understanding but now we don't need it anymore.*

> **Federica Bellagamba, rep. of user-groups:** *This is also the sensation I experienced...on the respect of the board metaphor. cause when Enrico translated the Web4Groups-interface into Italian for the exercise we did in April, I realised that some things that I read in English in Italian seemed to be different things, So I was a little bit confused....We tried to find messages, activities and so on... we the users had problems in getting a "mental interface".*

> **Researcher:** *.. as user model in your head...*

> **Federica Bellagamba, rep. of user-groups:** *Yes*

> **Alois Kempf, rep. of user-groups:** *I also think that we not fully have understood the board metaphor. and when I look back to reactions that we got during demonstrations and validations, the approach of the users were much like "what is like Eudora" or what is like another tool they were familiar with and we had to try to translate what the idea would mean to them. the persistence of what the user had in mind were much stronger... we were not successful in understanding the board metaphor and in translating it to the users.*

> **Jacob Palme, developer:** *If you are a good software-designer then you try to define the internal structure to be quite flexible with few and very general purpose ideas .... however from a user point of view this has not to be good, because users want concrete examples; users want to see forums and workspaces, they want to see users and messages, very concrete and understandable things...so it is a mistake to believe that the same model is the right one for software designers as for users.*

> **Valentino Cavalli, project-manager:** *Two lessons learned thanks to the use of the board metaphor: the one is the social aspect: the use of this metaphor was in a certain way innovative or provocative and raised very long discussions, and this discussion was really participated by the people and by different groups, users, developers - by people with different opinions. The other aspect is that this metaphor is half way between the view of the user and the view of a software-designer. So perhaps some problems in understanding the concept and in criticising the concept is also a necessary side-effect of this being half way between this different perspectives as Jacob very well said.*

**Conclusion for future co-operation:** The board metaphor was indeed a useful means within project-communication: it was a means to initiate and to support co-operation, but on the other hand it was not possible to convey it to the users. Users had severe problems in understanding it. - and beside that It was not possible to translate it in other languages. The interface may contain the term "board" in certain cases but it should not be used as a basic conceptual term for user interaction. Users should get very realistic and concrete concepts like messages, activities, forums and so on.

#### Scenarios

They are yet another technique to activate the imagination of team-members: Users and developers shall develop scenarios of future usage - how will their work and their communication look like when being supported by the new software.

> **Researcher:** *Was the scenario technique useful to you as developers:*

> **Samba Diallo, developer:** *It should have been. I don't think it was. There hasn't been any session between those who provided their scenarios and those who had to design the software. So I think people should sit together: the initiators of the scenarios should explain to the developers and some of them should be prototyped and implemented. There should be some kind of cycle going back to those who wrote the scenarios to check is my scenario makeable with the system now. #### Prototypes

Most scenarios are simply written descriptions, whereas the function of prototypes is to provide a more or less realistic simulation. The term prototype has a lot of meanings: There are wizard-of-oz-prototypes, drawn on some sheets of paper and on the other hand full-running prototypes without significant differences from running programmes. In Web4Groups we first used mock-ups with no functionality and very limited possibilities of interaction. Its main objective was to give a vivid impression of how the product would look like. There was a fixed sequence of windows to simulate how a certain task could be fulfilled using the system. Later the system itself performed certain prototype-functions.

By giving pictures instead of literal descriptions prototypes are perceived as more real than scenarios. Prototypes are more concrete than scenarios and can be discussed much better. Our experience in Web4Groups showed that papers as a means to transport ideas were not very successful, e.g. written specifications were not read or people had difficulties to understand them, whereas prototypes were broadly discussed and could be used by developers instead of written specifications.

Papers can only be read sequentially but a prototype is more an object that is perceived at one glance. One of the main advantages of prototypes is that a number of persons can follow a demonstration simultaneously. They are therefore a perfect means to stimulate discussions and to focus the participants' awareness.

### 7. Design-Settings

From our - the assessment-groups' - point of view we had the impression that design needs initiatives as well as social and organisational framework to be successful. We want to call these *design-settings*. Generally spoken the aim of design-settings is to support the flow of communication. But communication itself cannot be seen a success - it has to lead to results in the form of design-artifacts and products. In Web4Groups design-settings were set-up in the form of mailing lists, conferences and meetings, which will be discussed into detail later.

As resources (above all time and money) are always limited in software-development projects, one cannot wait that design-settings emerge by incident. They have to be planned. In the case of Web4Groups, the European Commission expected a detailed project-design as part of the contract with the Web4Groups-consortium. On the other hand planning often predetermines the project's evolution and sometimes leads to unexpected situations. In Web4Groups e.g. one of the main obstacles for internal communication seemed to be the spatial dispersion of partners over all Europe. It was tried to cope with that problem through intensive e-mail communication, telephone calls and telephone-conferences and also real conferences and meetings, which took place from time to time. However, our experience showed that more face-to-face meetings, would have been needed. It would have been needed an additional design setting where developers could co-operate intensively face-to-face for several weeks. At a later phase of the project it was not possible to establish such a design-setting anymore.

#### Management structure

The position of a technical manager was introduced at a relatively late stage, after almost one year since the project began. The technical manager had to perform also other roles in the project.

Answers to the questionnaire as well as informal talks showed that people had no problems with management in general but people want to improve information exchange especially between developers. A lot of them stressed the need for a strong technical management that co-ordinates debugging and new releases.

**Conclusions for future co-operation:** Technical management is a full-time job, and must be carried out in a very active way.

### 8. Project Assessment

It has to be the aim of all kind of project assessment to evaluate the process of management and design and to assure a high product quality. In most cases project-assessment does not intervene very much in the projects' process. In many cases assessment takes place at a rather late phase and looks back. It focuses more on products than on processes. The way project-assessment took place in Web4Groups was quite the contrary. It focused on the process-aspect of design and tried from the beginning to intervene actively. Assessment therefore took over some management-tasks and played an important role at the meetings. The assessment-manager was member of the management-board.

WSL claimed assessment to be eutral, goal-oriented and accepted by the project partners. In Web4Groups the assessment manager was wearing more than one hat.

The tasks of assessment in Web4Groups can be characterised as follows:
1st: To initiate and moderate the emergence of design-artifacts.
2nd: To support the emergence of design-settings to discuss and evaluate design-artifacts as well as products.

Such an idea of assessment is trying to provide an organisational framework for the evaluation of future developments. Assessment takes care of an equilibrium between inner dynamics and external resources: The results of design, namely the products, have to meet user-needs and expectations from the investors. If it does not, the process of the design and the whole medium will be in danger. The term medium points out a sense of responsibility that also includes settings of application e.g. on-line communities working with Web4Groups. The Assessment-group therefore established a demonstration-service and supported user-groups in the set-up of discussions to assure that developments are measured against real life requirements. The users' experiences should influence the next product versions.

In the long term assessment should shift from technical design to social design, i.e. to shape communities by giving them tools perfectly fitting to their needs. This will of course need more elaborated methods of assessment that have to be linked with new forms of democratic decision-making. A challenging task.

A few short comments in the discussion and informal talks showed that people mostly liked our work. People liked the active role of the assessment; it was especially mentioned that the assessment manager's visiting to the partners was fruitful for the internal communication flow.

WSL claimed assessment to be neutral, goal-oriented and accepted by the project partners. WSL slightly critisised that being involved in several tasks (like design and technical implementation) the assessment manager "was wearing more than one hat".

### 9. Conclusions and recommendations for future projects

One important result of the Web4Groups project is that there are two successor-projects namely the EC-funded projects *Select*, that deals with information-filtering and *Senior On-line*, focusing on elderly people in the Internet. Both projects will use Web4Groups as an important technical input.

**For the start-up phase of these projects we recommend to perform following actions:**

Give all partners the time and the possibility to demonstrate what they regard as their essential input for the project! Make presentations and tutorials. E.g. DSV should get the possibility to talk about the history and the origin of the KOM-System and should give a tutorial of SuperKOM.

Also Web4Groups - of course - should be presented into detail. New partners should get the possibility to make first experiences supported by people who already know the system. (I also want to point to Jacob Palme's paper on Multi-Country Coding that provide a lot of more valuable ideas how to start a project)

Provide general scenarios of the future technical and cultural development of the Internet especially of Internet Browsers and WWW-Services! Imagine how computer-mediated communication will look like when the project will be over! Think about how Web4Groups could look like and what particular functions it will perform in the Internet two years from now!

**Recommendations for internal co-operation:**

Enable members of the consortium, especially developers to travel and to spend not only days but one to two weeks together with other partners. It turned out in the Web4Groups project that severe and complex problems, especially problems where more than two people were involved, could only be solved during face to face-meetings.

Project-communication should be based on Web4Groups. For people who are used to mailing lists this will result in some changes of their communication behaviour. This process should be accompanied with tutorials and moderation. People should slowly get used to this new form of communication. Problems should be discussed in regular face-to-face meetings.

However we assume that people will appreciate the advantages of Web4Groups when it is used as a "real groupware system" enabling people to split up continuously into new discussions and discussion-groups.

Use also other means of communication like e.g. telephone conferences if face-to-face meetings are currently not possible. Telephone conferences are an appropriate means of co-operation if people already know each other (know their faces and above all their voices), best at a later phase of the project. Not more than four to five persons should participate in the conference. The agenda should be rather short. For example certain technical issues could be clarified in such a telephone conference. One person should chair the conference. The same or another person should make notes in an IRC channel which can be read and amended by all participants.

**Management and Decision-Making:**

In the Web4Groups project it turned out that long discussions especially discussions on mailing lists didn't end up in decision. Certain rules should be defined in the start-up phase in order to come to decisions. E.g. The moderator or the organiser of a discussion should summarise certain options and start a vote. In important cases a person should be appointed in order to co-ordinate the execution of that decision. Technical management should take over the moderation of most important technical issues.

Technical management turned out to be a key-position in the Web4Groups project. It should be a full-time job and carried out in a very active way. The technical manager should maintain the task-lists and the bug data-base and co-ordinate all developing efforts. It is important that s/he - together with other developers - sets up certain methods e.g. how to release new versions or how to fix bugs. These methods have to be followed by all partners. Technical management should make a clever release-policy, which results in stable versions. Don't expand functionality in undebugged versions!

Jacob Palme lists in his paper following tasks:
1. To have a full technical knowledge of the tasks done by all the developers at all geographical sites.
2. To be impartial in disagreements, to try to solve problems by understanding each person's views and problems, and trying to find solutions which all can accept. Critical in this task is understanding.
3. To actively follow what happens at all developing sites.
4. To keep the task list, see separate segment below.
5. To use e-mail, telephone, and, when needed, travel to the different groups to talk to people and understand their problems.
6. To summarise discussions and issues and propose solutions.
7. To stop discussions on issues which are fully clarified and where solutions, not more discussion is needed.
8. To ensure that all developers are all the time aware of which are the most urgent tasks for them. This can be done, for example, by making a list of the most important tasks to be done by each developer and sending this list once a week (but covering several weeks of work) since changes will often occur in the priorities on the list.

**Linking Users and Developers**

When using scenario-techniques prototypes, metaphors and the like, assure that all team-members know the results. When using methods of user-participation set-up a procedure how to communicate the results within the team! Consider the effects of each method for internal communication! Set-up methods in respect to its functions for the communication between users and developers!
